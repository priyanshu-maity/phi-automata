**Φ Automata** is a research and engineering project that explores how complex behavior emerges from simple rules. At its core, the project simulates cellular automata and measures their behavior using several independent complexity metrics.

The goal is not simply to visualize evolving patterns. It is to investigate whether different mathematical theories of emergence, computation, and cognition identify the *same* regions of interest within a system.

<br>

> **Central Question**
>
> *When does a collection of simple cells begin to exhibit behavior that looks computationally or cognitively significant?*

<br>

By running multiple complexity measures on the same substrate, Φ Automata aims to build a unified framework for studying emergence at the edge of chaos.

<br>

---

## &nbsp;Motivation

Cellular automata are among the simplest systems capable of producing rich and unexpected behavior. A grid of cells following local rules can generate persistent structures, self-organization, information transfer, and universal computation.

Several theories have attempted to explain why certain systems appear more *complex* or more *intelligent* than others, yet these frameworks are typically studied in isolation:

<br>

<div align="center">

| Theory | Symbol | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated Information Theory | **Φ** | Information generated beyond its parts |
| Compression Complexity | **Φ\[C\]** | Algorithmic information as proxy for integration |
| Excess Information & Synergy | **εI** | Irreducible collective behavior |
| Criticality & Edge of Chaos | **λ** | Order–chaos transition boundaries |

</div>

<br>

**Φ Automata brings them together** within a single experimental environment and compares their behavior directly.

<br>

## &nbsp;Core Instruments

<br>

### `Φ` &nbsp; Integrated Information

Measures how much information is generated by a system *beyond* the contribution of its individual parts. High Φ suggests strong integration across the system, a hallmark of tightly coupled, non-decomposable dynamics.

<br>

### `Φ[C]` &nbsp; Compression Complexity

Uses lossless compression as a practical approximation of integrated information. Patterns that are neither completely ordered nor completely random tend to exhibit higher complexity, compression becomes a thermometer for structure.

<br>

### `εI` &nbsp; Excess Information

Identifies regions where collective behavior contains information that cannot be reduced to individual components. Useful for detecting emergent structures that only exist at the level of the whole.

<br>

### `λ` &nbsp; Criticality Analysis

Uses measures such as Langton's λ and Derrida analysis to identify transitions between order and chaos. Many complex computational systems appear near these transition boundaries, the edge of chaos as a design principle.

## &nbsp;Research Questions

These are the open questions driving the project:

1. Do different complexity measures identify the *same* regions of a system?
2. Does compression complexity provide a reliable proxy for integrated information?
3. Which automata rules maximize emergence?
4. What occurs near the edge of chaos?
5. Can persistent structures be detected automatically from information-theoretic measures?

<br>

## &nbsp;Current Status

This project is under active development.

The first milestone is a cellular automata engine capable of simulating and visualizing rule based systems efficiently. Subsequent milestones will introduce complexity metrics, emergence detection, and large scale experimental analysis.

---

## &nbsp;Long-Term Vision

Φ Automata is intended to become a **computational observatory** for studying emergence across artificial worlds.

The broader goal is to develop tools that help answer fundamental questions about complexity, computation, and the conditions under which cognition may arise from simple interacting components.
